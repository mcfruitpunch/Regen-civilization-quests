# REGEN — Full Foundation Framework v0.1

Version 0.1, 2026-10-09. Working draft; not a deployed civic platform.

This is the combined text edition of the project foundation. The GitHub-ready ZIP also contains the prototype, quest data, schema, and tests.


---

# 00 · Founding Charter

## North star

Make regenerative action discoverable, learnable, collaborative, and measurably beneficial—from a first 10-minute activity to long-term changes in institutions and infrastructure. REGEN is not a political party, moral ranking system, or substitute for democratic processes. It is a participation and learning infrastructure.

## The problem

Civilizational problems feel too large and abstract. Helpful efforts are fragmented; people do not always know where to start, what they can contribute, whether a proposed solution works, or how an individual effort connects to systemic change. Adversarial narratives can turn reform into permanent identity conflict. Incumbent organizations may feel threatened, while people harmed by those organizations are told to wait for voluntary improvement.

## Founding promises

1. **Human dignity is unconditional.** Disagreement does not make someone disposable. No person is a quest objective to be manipulated or 'fixed.'
2. **Accountability is non-negotiable.** Invite change, but do not trade away safety, rights, remediation, or enforcement.
3. **Useful action beats ideological conformity.** A quest rewards learning and contribution, not affirming a worldview.
4. **Prove benefits, surface costs.** Show how evidence was collected; distinguish claims, self-reports, reviewed findings, and independently validated outcomes.
5. **Local knowledge, global learning.** Communities define priorities; reusable methods travel with their context and limitations.
6. **No coercion or covert manipulation.** Participation, recruitment, and institutional dialogue are voluntary and honest. The system does not coordinate infiltration, deception, harassment, or sabotage.
7. **Privacy and safety by default.** Collect only what a task needs; many quests require no account or data submission.
8. **Accessible from the beginning.** Offline, remote, low-cost, disability-accessible, and no-purchase pathways matter as much as visible public activities.
9. **Repair over purity.** People and institutions can demonstrate good-faith change through measurable, independently reviewed progress and remedy for past harm.
10. **Power must remain challengeable.** No founder, organization, donor, or algorithm is above scrutiny, appeals, or an appropriate removal process.

## A more careful version of 'us fixes them'

REGEN separates **people**, **harmful behavior**, and **harm-producing systems**. People can learn and cooperate; behaviors can be stopped and sanctioned; systems can be redesigned, democratized, or replaced when justified. This includes examining assumptions and blind spots within REGEN itself. We are all inside the systems being transformed.

## What 'regenerative' means here

A change is regenerative when, relative to a meaningful baseline, it helps restore or expand long-term ecological health, human capabilities, trust, shared prosperity, institutional accountability, or resilience **without hiding its costs elsewhere**. No single indicator can prove the whole concept. Benefits and harms must be assessed with affected people, including where effects may be displaced.

## Core outcome lenses

- **Ecological:** waste, pollution, energy/material impacts, habitat, water, restoration.
- **Human well-being:** health, safety, access, agency, time, stability.
- **Social:** mutual aid, trust, inclusion, fair distribution of benefits/burdens.
- **Economic:** livelihood quality, repairability, local resilience, avoidable costs.
- **Institutional:** transparency, accountability, informed participation, due process.
- **Learning:** transferable skills, documented lessons, mistakes and replication limits.

## Participation rights

Opt out without punishment; know why a quest was recommended; refuse to upload evidence; challenge a quest's safety or framing; request removal of submitted information (when data storage eventually exists); see sponsorship and conflict-of-interest disclosures; participate without revealing political beliefs. Minors require tailored safeguards and age-appropriate participation before any live account system.

# 01 · The Game: From First Step to Global Network

## Design goal

Give people a satisfying path from curiosity to meaningful contribution **without reducing civic life to points**. The game metaphor supplies clarity, feedback, collaboration, and momentum; people do not become avatars whose value is assigned by a score.

## Seven scopes, not social ranks

| Scope | Name | Main question | Example | Advance when... |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The Awakening | What can I do? | Identify neighborhood strengths and a skill to explore | Learner can navigate, consent, and reflect |
| 2 | First Seeds | What works in practice? | Complete an accessible, low-risk local or remote action | An action and lesson are documented |
| 3 | The Party Forms | What can we do together? | Coordinate a small, opt-in improvement | Team has clear roles and consent |
| 4 | The Village | Can we sustain an improvement? | Run a community-approved pilot | Measures show benefits and burdens |
| 5 | System Builders | How do institutions change? | Co-design a purchasing or accessibility improvement | Affected stakeholders review progress |
| 6 | Great Transformation | Can proven practices scale responsibly? | Adapt the pilot across organizations | Trade-offs and replication are assessed |
| 7 | The Living World | How do networks learn globally? | Share openly reviewed playbooks across regions | Local autonomy and feedback remain intact |

These are different **scopes**, not earned human status. A participant can work across scopes; prerequisites apply only when needed for safety, consent, or competence. Return paths, coaching and restarting are normal.

## Core loop

**Explore → Choose → Prepare → Act → Reflect → Validate at the appropriate level → Share learnings (optional) → Discover next quest.**

## Skill domains

Ecological restoration; community care; engineering and repair; civic literacy and governance; creative storytelling; education and research; regenerative enterprise; systems mapping. Skills are demonstrated through optional projects, mentorship, and learning portfolios—not assigned based on identity, wealth, educational credentials, or political views.

## Quest classes

- **Learn:** build competence and context. Completion: reflective check, not ideological agreement.
- **Observe:** gather a respectful, privacy-safe baseline with permission when required.
- **Act:** test a bounded, reversible improvement.
- **Collaborate:** bring people together around a concrete, consensual task.
- **Transform:** work through a transparent, lawful institutional-change plan with stakeholder participation and accountability.

## Required quest card fields

Purpose, scope, skill domains, intended beneficiaries, prerequisites, time/cost/access alternatives, instructions, consent requirements, specific safety exclusions, completion claim, evidence tier, possible unintended consequences, escalation/feedback path, and related Atlas node(s) when available.

## Meaningful progression without toxic gamification

Reward **learning loops** and **quality of documented contributions**, not raw activity volume, popularity, ideological alignment, recruitment numbers, or public callouts. No competitive rankings of people, organizations, or neighborhoods in the MVP. An optional private progress indicator should clearly say that completion is *self-reported activity*, not an independently verified social impact metric.

## Participation paths

A 10-minute at-home quest, a quiet observation walk, a sensory-friendly group task, translation of public information, and an in-person build day should all be equally legitimate ways to begin. Never require public-facing work, spending money, unsafe labor, or personal disclosure to make progress.

## Sample starter questline: Less Food Waste

1. Learn common food-waste sources and distinguish avoidable waste from safety-required disposal.
2. With consent, track an anonymous baseline in a home or participating organization.
3. Test one low-risk prevention adjustment without compromising food safety or worker conditions.
4. Review results with staff and affected people; publish only aggregated, permissioned findings.
5. If promising, propose an accountable and reversible operations pilot, then share a replicable playbook.

**Stop condition:** If a quest risks privacy, food safety, labor rights, or retaliation, do not proceed until its design is changed or proper oversight is obtained.

# 02 · Quest Engine and Evidence Model

## Purpose

Translate community-identified needs and vetted action pathways into *relevant, feasible, accessible, safe* quest suggestions. This engine is a recommendation assistant—not an authority determining what a good citizen should believe or do.

## Inputs: v0 and beyond

- **Required:** chosen interest or browse-all; basic quest metadata; human-reviewed safety classification.
- **Optional:** time available, at-home/in-person preference, accessibility requirements, skill interests, collaborator preference.
- **Later, only with explicit consent:** approximate region to recommend locally relevant opportunities. No precise location required by default.
- **Contextual:** consented community priorities, Atlas system nodes, known resource constraints, actual measurable outcomes, feedback on failed interventions.

## Deterministic MVP matching

**Gate before ranking.** Reject quests requiring unavailable permissions, dangerous actions, disallowed targeting, or missing human review. For remaining quests, use transparent filters and a simple relevance sort. A future ranking model may weight feasibility, access, community-requested importance, learning fit, expected benefit, and uncertainty. Always show *why* a quest appears and allow manual browsing and challenge.

Proposed future score for experiments only (weights require stakeholder testing):

`fit = 0.30 * feasibility + 0.25 * accessibility + 0.20 * community_priority + 0.15 * skill_interest + 0.10 * evidence_quality`

Scores must never substitute for safety gates or become a moral rating of people. Predicted outcomes should not be described as realized impact.

## Evidence tiers

| Tier | Label | Meaning | Appropriate UI language |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E0 | Unchecked activity | User has begun or marked a step; no proof | "Self-reported activity" |
| E1 | Participant reflection | Reflection or personal baseline exists | "Documented by participant" |
| E2 | Reviewed observation | Evidence reviewed under published rubric | "Reviewed, with limitations" |
| E3 | Independent outcome | Credible independent verification and baseline comparison | "Verified outcome, scope specified" |

No quest completion automatically upgrades a claim to E2 or E3. Different evidence types are necessary for different tasks; low-stakes learning does not need high-stakes verification. Never demand identifying photos or public testimony simply to award a badge.

## Quest publishing pipeline (future)

Proposal → structured lint → consent and impact review → risk tier assignment → accessibility review → pilot → revision → community governance approval → versioned release → incident reporting and retirement. High-risk or institution-focused quests always require named accountable human reviewers and legal-context evaluation before deployment.

## What the engine must learn

Record uncertainty, unintended harms, missing context, burden shifted onto volunteers, and differences across communities. A quest that succeeds in one location is a hypothesis for another location, not an instruction to copy without adaptation. Keep a reversible version history.

## Red lines

No covert surveillance; gathering private dossiers; political profiling; automated mass persuasion; deception; harassment; trespassing; retaliation; doxxing; unsafe physical activity; unlicensed regulated work. Do not create quests to 'convert' an unwilling person. Opt-in dialogue is allowed; accountability and lawful public processes remain available.

# 03 · The Bridge Protocol: Institutional Transformation Without Dehumanization

**The principle:** Invite every person to participate in change; protect people from harm while that invitation remains open.

The protocol targets **repairable practices and incentives**, not identity groups. It does not require victims to reconcile with perpetrators, pardon misconduct, or accept continued exposure to harm.

## Ten steps

1. **Map the system.** Identify the public problem, root incentives, dependencies, relevant law, existing alternatives, and uncertainty. Avoid caricatures of opponents.
2. **Define the harm.** Specify whose rights, health, livelihoods, or environment are affected and what evidence supports the concern.
3. **Hear affected people first.** Seek consented input and avoid requiring people to relive harm to participate.
4. **Set non-negotiable safeguards.** Safety, due process, nondiscrimination, privacy, labor protections, and remedy are not bargaining chips.
5. **Offer a credible path.** Present feasible changes, transition support, fair incentives, operational constraints, and a reversible pilot where appropriate.
6. **Invite transparent participation.** Engage organization leaders, workers, community representatives, regulators, and independent experts openly—without deception or pressure campaigns against private individuals.
7. **Make commitments verifiable.** Put measurable milestones, time frames, disclosures, and responsible parties into a public or appropriately governed compact.
8. **Pilot and independently review.** Compare outcomes against an honest baseline; include distribution of benefits and burdens, not just headline success.
9. **Repair and escalate as necessary.** If violations continue or participants refuse genuine accountability, use proportionate lawful options: internal grievance processes, community organizing, union processes, regulators, litigation, democratic advocacy, or alternative service provision. Do not wait indefinitely for voluntary reform.
10. **Reassess and re-enter.** Institutions can regain trust through demonstrated action, remediation, monitoring, and affected-party input; praise or forgiveness cannot be purchased with donations or rhetoric.

## Example: a wasteful commercial food operation

**Not:** "Expose this owner as the enemy." **Instead:** Establish a permission-based baseline; propose safe waste-reduction measures; include workers in design; measure costs and benefits; offer a transition compact; publicly share only verified, non-sensitive findings. If a serious labor, food safety, or environmental violation is discovered, prioritize reporting and protecting people through proper channels rather than completing the gamified quest.

## Power and control safeguards

- Give affected communities meaningful representation and an independent complaint channel.
- Disclose conflicts of interest, donor incentives, and who controls measurement.
- Independent monitors must be able to publish unfavorable results.
- Prevent self-certification of 'redemption'; progress badges never erase liabilities.
- Require explicit consent before naming an organization as a voluntary participant.
- Public criticism of institutions is legitimate; coordinated personal targeting and intimidation are not.

## What counts as a successful bridge?

Evidence of reduced harm and improved conditions, with affected-party validation and ongoing accountability. **Agreement is not success. Compliance theater is not success. Behavior and outcomes matter.**

# 04 · Safety, Anti-Capture, Privacy, Governance

## Risk register (seed)

| Failure mode | Early signal | Initial control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Co-optation by donors or public figures | Sponsored quests favor sponsor claims | Transparent funding, conflict recusal, independent review, appeals |
| Greenwashing/redemption-washing | Awards for promises rather than results | No institutional certification without independent evidence + affected input |
| Harassment of opponents | Named people become quest 'targets' | Prohibit targeting, no leaderboards, reporting + moderation |
| Covert infiltration or retaliation | Participants asked to misrepresent roles or gather private information | Prohibit covert tasks; offer lawful public/consented alternatives |
| Participant surveillance | Identity/location collection grows | Data minimization, local-first MVP, granular consent, deletion and retention limits |
| Manipulated impact claims | Rewarding volume without verification | Evidence tiers, auditable methods, outcome audits, rollback |
| Exclusion or unpaid extraction | Quests require money/physical ability or burden vulnerable groups | Accessibility alternatives, compensation policy, consent, equity review |
| Unsafe real-world activity | Food handling, construction, cleanup without oversight | Domain-specific guidance; human review; liability/insurance evaluation |
| Institutional capture of governance | One stakeholder controls moderation, funds, and verification | Independent roles, term limits/rotation, public decisions, appeal routes |
| Cultural imposition | Imported playbooks override local knowledge | Local co-design, adaptation rights, indigenous/community governance |
| Reputational scoring | XP treated as moral worth or opportunity gate | No global human rank, no credit/employment eligibility function |
| Misinformation | Unchecked claims presented as facts | Sourced context, correction history, review before scaling |

## Governance architecture to test

- **Local circles:** opt-in community bodies select priorities and review locally deployed tasks.
- **Stewardship council:** maintains charter, publishing standards, transparency reports, and review procedures; includes independent affected-community representation.
- **Safety and appeals panel:** independent ability to pause quests, review abuse reports, and overturn decisions.
- **Evidence reviewers:** separate from quest sponsors and operators wherever feasible; publish methods and conflict disclosures.

Avoid proposing final voting allocation until pilots reveal whose interests are affected. Incorporate accessibility and language access. Require public, documented decisions about material changes to the quest charter.

## Privacy principles

Local progress requires no account. No default GPS collection, identity verification, social graph, or evidence uploads. Future collaborative features need purpose limits, threat modeling, clear retention and deletion, role-based access, safety review, and secure incident response. Public reporting should use aggregated results and suppress identifying small groups. Never expose whistleblowers or participants who may face retaliation.

## Safety gates for proposed quests

A quest is withheld if it asks for unsafe/illegal activity, unconsented access, political or personal profiling, discriminatory targeting, collecting nonpublic personal information, public accusation without process, medical/legal guidance beyond competence, or physical activities requiring unavailable expertise. Moderate/higher-risk pilot work needs local subject-matter review and informed consent.

## Complaints and incident handling (target workflow)

Report → immediate safety triage → temporary pause if needed → investigator without conflict → affected persons heard without forcing disclosure → documented decision and remedy → appeal → learnings incorporated without exposing victims.

**v0.1 status:** These are design requirements, not mechanisms already implemented by the offline demo.

# 05 · Roadmap, Pilot, and Definition of Done

## First north-star problem

Can a new participant go from "I want to help" to **one useful, safe, accessible action**, and understand its limitations and next step, without joining an ideological group or exposing private information?

## Phase 0 — Foundation (this package)

- [x] Charter, three-system model, seven scopes, core quest schema
- [x] Bridge Protocol and safety/governance initial drafts
- [x] Twelve example quests and offline proof-of-concept interface
- [x] Local-only self-reported progress, not impact verification
- [ ] External safety review, affected-community review, and usability testing

## Phase 1 — Narrow participatory pilot (approx. first 30 days)

Choose one consenting group and **one** issue (for example, avoidable food waste in a participating community kitchen or business). Co-design 5–8 quests spanning learn/observe/act/collaborate. Recruit a small diverse pilot cohort; offer at-home and no-cost options. Establish baseline, participant support, and incident/opt-out channels. Do not publish organization-level comparisons.

**Exit criteria:** Participant understands next action, knows limits/consent requirements, can opt out, and has a documented reflection; no material unresolved safety findings.

## Phase 2 — Validate effects (roughly days 30–60)

Run consented experiments; track adoption, abandonment, accessibility obstacles, work burden, and measurable outcome changes versus baseline. Seek independent review for claims beyond participant self-report. Test whether invitations to existing institutions are welcomed or feel coercive.

**Exit criteria:** At least one pilot demonstrates a credible benefit without an unacceptable burden shift, plus transparent documentation of failures and limits.

## Phase 3 — Repeatable open starter (roughly days 60–90)

Version quests, add human review workflow, publish sample data contract and onboarding materials, test with a second distinct community, and decide initial governance and licensing. Create a dedicated GitHub repo and issue board when ownership and contribution rules are chosen.

**Exit criteria:** A stranger can run the prototype, a moderator can reject a risky quest, a participant can challenge a claim, and another community can adapt the pilot without needing founder permission.

## Measures that matter

- Median time from arrival to completing a first **safe** action.
- Accessible task completion by participation mode (reported in aggregated, privacy-safe form).
- Return rate for second action without pressure or dark patterns.
- Outcomes measured against an appropriate baseline, with uncertainty.
- Complaints, harms, and hours of uncompensated participant burden.
- Number of contested or corrected impact claims, and resolution time.
- Institutional changes actually maintained after a pilot ends.

**Anti-metrics:** follower count, ideological conversion, badge accumulation, number of 'opponents' persuaded, or number of institutions publicly embarrassed.

## GitHub handoff checklist

Repository name suggestion: `regen-civilization-quests` (not yet created). Confirm project ownership, license, contributor guidelines, code of conduct, privacy disclosure, maintainers, moderation capacity, community partnership agreements, and how the Atlas interfaces. Then push this starter as an initial `foundation-v0.1` commit. Connect a static hosting preview only after content safety and public identity considerations are reviewed.

# 06 · Technical Architecture (proposed)

## Design posture

Open formats, modular components, local-first onboarding, explicit consent, adaptable for federation. A simple static offline prototype is appropriate now; do not build data-heavy infrastructure before pilot demand exists.

## Conceptual modules

1. **Atlas adapter:** maps current/transition/regenerative nodes and their evidence into quest references. Read-only until a review pathway exists.
2. **Quest catalog:** versioned structured content, safety status, accessibility, prerequisites, localization, outcomes, evidence tier.
3. **Recommendation layer:** explainable, optional filters; human override; deterministic minimum viable implementation.
4. **Local participant experience:** browsing, quests, reflections, accessible alternatives, local progress. No sign-in needed for solo MVP.
5. **Group coordination:** future opt-in team tasks, consent, transparent roles, anti-harassment controls.
6. **Impact and evidence:** store methodology and outcome claims separately from activity checkboxes; permissioned review and corrections.
7. **Bridge workflow:** institutional commitments, stakeholder sign-off, public milestones, independent findings and escalation paths.
8. **Governance and trust:** appeals, safeguarding, audit log, transparent funding and conflicts.

## Trust boundaries

Quest authors cannot self-certify their own high-impact results. Sponsors cannot exclusively control evidence reviews. Recommendation code cannot silently change eligibility rules. Institution participants cannot see individual participant data without consent. Public narratives must not leak sensitive evidence. Community stewardship must remain contestable.

## Data objects (concept)

- `Quest`: version, type, stage, safety/consent requirements, instructions, accessibility, evidence requirement.
- `QuestRun`: local user instance, state, optional private reflection. In demo, only completion IDs are persisted.
- `OutcomeClaim`: linked quest run/pilot, metrics, baseline, period, methodology, confidence/uncertainty.
- `EvidenceReview`: author, reviewer independence, findings, conflicts, limitations, dispute status.
- `AtlasNode`: external reference + relation types and version, not copied blindly.
- `BridgeCompact`: participating institution, affected-party governance, commitments, remedies, milestones, independent monitor.
- `GovernanceDecision`: proposal, consultation, vote/decision procedure, conflict disclosures, appeals.

## Data interoperability

Prefer JSON Schema for quest records, documented API interfaces, exportable content, localization keys, and a stable URI convention for Atlas links. A quest may connect to multiple Atlas nodes through `atlas_refs` after identifiers and versioning are agreed. Avoid locked proprietary recommendation logic; publish safety/ranking criteria in human-readable terms.

## Prototype implementation

`web/index.html` is a standalone browser app generated from the same `data/quests.json` seed. It stores completed quest IDs in browser localStorage only, with no network calls. The included generator `tests/build_prototype.py` refreshes the embedded dataset. The tests validate every quest against the schema and check basic internal links and IDs. This is **not** an institutional verification engine or production service.

# 07 · Decisions, Hypotheses, and Open Questions

## Locked for the concept foundation

- Emphasize practical regenerative outcomes over ideological recruitment.
- Protect people's dignity while holding actions/institutions accountable.
- Use seven **scopes**, not castes or moral levels.
- Make introductory participation possible without accounts, money, or precise location.
- Separate personal progress from externally verified impact.
- Prohibit coercion, covert infiltration, profiling, deception, and harassment.
- Treat local governance and existing knowledge as essential, not implementation details.

## Hypotheses to falsify

1. Quest framing reduces the anxiety of starting real-world community work.
2. Private skill progression motivates sustained contribution without social-score effects.
3. Explicit alternatives help people with different access needs join meaningfully.
4. A measured, good-faith invitation makes some incumbent organizations more willing to improve.
5. Independently measured outcomes are more credible than public badges, even if harder to produce.
6. A federated Atlas/quest design can scale without centralizing moral or political authority.

## Questions deliberately unresolved

- What is the best brand/name? REGEN is a working title.
- Who legally owns the platform, and how can mission drift be resisted?
- Which open-source license suits software; which license suits community-created content?
- How do local communities choose authorized reviewers and handle difficult disagreement?
- What is the appeals standard when a quest challenges an institution's conduct?
- What measures distinguish a genuinely regenerative outcome from displaced costs?
- Should completion points exist at all, or would private portfolios and milestones be healthier?
- How should participants be paid for significant labor, especially when companies benefit?
- How do we protect whistleblowers, minors, and participants in authoritarian environments?
- How can Atlas knowledge be represented without implying certainty or one global worldview?

## First design experiment

Run 5 structured usability sessions on the offline demo: one participant with 10 minutes only, one remote participant, one person who distrusts game mechanics, one community organizer, and one staff member from an interested institution. Ask each participant to find a quest they would voluntarily attempt, identify what is being claimed, explain how to opt out, and surface missing prerequisites. Revise the experience **before** asking for any real-world evidence.
