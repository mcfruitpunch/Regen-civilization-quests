# 11 · Licensing and Governance Decision Record (PROPOSAL ONLY)

**Status:** unapproved options for maintainer and affected-community consideration. No choice here grants any permission or constitutes a legal review.

## Why decide now?

REGEN's source, world-building materials, quest descriptions, and community contributions have different reuse risks. Public GitHub visibility alone does **not** make the work open source. Publishing actual licenses without considering contributor ownership and community trust could reduce future autonomy.

## Proposed software license shortlist

| Choice | Advantages | Trade-offs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Apache License 2.0 | Broad modification and distribution rights; explicit patent license; familiar to commercial and nonprofit users | Allows proprietary versions that do not contribute improvements back |
| MIT | Simple, broadly compatible, low overhead | Permits proprietary forks; no express Apache-style patent clause |
| GNU AGPLv3 | Network-service source availability provisions strengthen reciprocity and can deter closed hosted forks | Some institutions and developers may avoid it; compatibility, compliance and deployment need more expertise |

**Decision question:** Is it more important to maximize adoption by many organizations, or preserve software reciprocity even at the cost of some adoption? There is no universally correct answer.

## Documentation and quest content

Documentation, illustrations, translations, and human-authored quest text may benefit from a *separate* content license. Options for rights holders to assess include CC BY 4.0 (attribution) and CC BY-SA 4.0 (attribution plus share-alike).

Do not assume user-contributed content is owned by REGEN. Contributors should control whether their contributions may be reused, and the agreement should be plainly understandable. Do not conflate consent to participate in a quest with permission to license someone's personal story, likeness, workplace information, or cultural knowledge.

**Decision question:** Will community groups be able to withdraw or restrict sensitive locally authored materials even when generic quest schemas remain reusable?

## An interim governance model to consider

### Stage A — Maintainer stewardship (before pilot)

- One designated maintainer can merge code and publish non-sensitive concept documentation.
- Publication of **real-world quests** is disabled until a review group, incident escalation, and participant-consent rules exist.
- Financial support, endorsements, and sponsor proposals are disclosed before influencing priorities.
- All decisions with material safety or public-interest impact receive a written rationale and conflict-of-interest check.
- There is no promise of 24/7 moderation, legal support, or emergency services.

### Stage B — Consented pilot (only after safeguards)

- Include willing, directly affected community members in determining needs, accessibility, impact measures, and exit criteria.
- Require explicit authorization by any participating institution; workers and vulnerable participants must have protected ways to decline.
- A named reviewer can stop or roll back a quest; there must also be an appeal or independent complaint route.
- Collect only minimum aggregated evidence and maintain retention/deletion rules.
- An advisory council is not a democratic mandate over people outside the pilot.

### Stage C — Federated participation (future proposal)

- Local groups retain autonomy over adaptations and participation.
- No central leader can sell civic standing, rank people's worth, or compel ideological conformity.
- Public audits examine outcomes, distributed costs, power concentration, harms, and corrections.
- Transparent versioning and forkability reduce capture risks, subject to compatible licensing.
- National/global coordination should be evidence-sharing rather than command over local communities.

## Red-line governance constraints

1. All quests remain opt-in; no coercion, political profiling, targeted persuasion, covert access, or doxxing.
2. Affected people can criticize or contest claims without losing access to public services or community aid.
3. Claims about a company's rehabilitation are based on independently reviewed changes and available remedy for harms, never sponsorship.
4. No sponsor or founder can override consent, safety, legal compliance, or the right to report wrongdoing.
5. Project leadership is accountable to the same standards as every participating institution.
