# 07 · Decisions, Hypotheses, and Open Questions

## Locked for the concept foundation

- Emphasize practical regenerative outcomes over ideological recruitment.
- Protect people's dignity while holding actions/institutions accountable.
- Use seven **scopes**, not castes or moral levels.
- Make introductory participation possible without accounts, money, or precise location.
- Separate personal progress from externally verified impact.
- Prohibit coercion, covert infiltration, profiling, deception, and harassment.
- Treat local governance and existing knowledge as essential, not implementation details.

## Hypotheses to falsify

1. Quest framing reduces the anxiety of starting real-world community work.
2. Private skill progression motivates sustained contribution without social-score effects.
3. Explicit alternatives help people with different access needs join meaningfully.
4. A measured, good-faith invitation makes some incumbent organizations more willing to improve.
5. Independently measured outcomes are more credible than public badges, even if harder to produce.
6. A federated Atlas/quest design can scale without centralizing moral or political authority.

## Questions deliberately unresolved

- What is the best brand/name? REGEN is a working title.
- Who legally owns the platform, and how can mission drift be resisted?
- Which open-source license suits software; which license suits community-created content?
- How do local communities choose authorized reviewers and handle difficult disagreement?
- What is the appeals standard when a quest challenges an institution's conduct?
- What measures distinguish a genuinely regenerative outcome from displaced costs?
- Should completion points exist at all, or would private portfolios and milestones be healthier?
- How should participants be paid for significant labor, especially when companies benefit?
- How do we protect whistleblowers, minors, and participants in authoritarian environments?
- How can Atlas knowledge be represented without implying certainty or one global worldview?

## First design experiment

Run 5 structured usability sessions on the offline demo: one participant with 10 minutes only, one remote participant, one person who distrusts game mechanics, one community organizer, and one staff member from an interested institution. Ask each participant to find a quest they would voluntarily attempt, identify what is being claimed, explain how to opt out, and surface missing prerequisites. Revise the experience **before** asking for any real-world evidence.
