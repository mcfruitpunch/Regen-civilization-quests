# 01 · The Game: From First Step to Global Network

## Design goal

Give people a satisfying path from curiosity to meaningful contribution **without reducing civic life to points**. The game metaphor supplies clarity, feedback, collaboration, and momentum; people do not become avatars whose value is assigned by a score.

## Seven scopes, not social ranks

| Scope | Name | Main question | Example | Advance when... |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The Awakening | What can I do? | Identify neighborhood strengths and a skill to explore | Learner can navigate, consent, and reflect |
| 2 | First Seeds | What works in practice? | Complete an accessible, low-risk local or remote action | An action and lesson are documented |
| 3 | The Party Forms | What can we do together? | Coordinate a small, opt-in improvement | Team has clear roles and consent |
| 4 | The Village | Can we sustain an improvement? | Run a community-approved pilot | Measures show benefits and burdens |
| 5 | System Builders | How do institutions change? | Co-design a purchasing or accessibility improvement | Affected stakeholders review progress |
| 6 | Great Transformation | Can proven practices scale responsibly? | Adapt the pilot across organizations | Trade-offs and replication are assessed |
| 7 | The Living World | How do networks learn globally? | Share openly reviewed playbooks across regions | Local autonomy and feedback remain intact |

These are different **scopes**, not earned human status. A participant can work across scopes; prerequisites apply only when needed for safety, consent, or competence. Return paths, coaching and restarting are normal.

## Core loop

**Explore → Choose → Prepare → Act → Reflect → Validate at the appropriate level → Share learnings (optional) → Discover next quest.**

## Skill domains

Ecological restoration; community care; engineering and repair; civic literacy and governance; creative storytelling; education and research; regenerative enterprise; systems mapping. Skills are demonstrated through optional projects, mentorship, and learning portfolios—not assigned based on identity, wealth, educational credentials, or political views.

## Quest classes

- **Learn:** build competence and context. Completion: reflective check, not ideological agreement.
- **Observe:** gather a respectful, privacy-safe baseline with permission when required.
- **Act:** test a bounded, reversible improvement.
- **Collaborate:** bring people together around a concrete, consensual task.
- **Transform:** work through a transparent, lawful institutional-change plan with stakeholder participation and accountability.

## Required quest card fields

Purpose, scope, skill domains, intended beneficiaries, prerequisites, time/cost/access alternatives, instructions, consent requirements, specific safety exclusions, completion claim, evidence tier, possible unintended consequences, escalation/feedback path, and related Atlas node(s) when available.

## Meaningful progression without toxic gamification

Reward **learning loops** and **quality of documented contributions**, not raw activity volume, popularity, ideological alignment, recruitment numbers, or public callouts. No competitive rankings of people, organizations, or neighborhoods in the MVP. An optional private progress indicator should clearly say that completion is *self-reported activity*, not an independently verified social impact metric.

## Participation paths

A 10-minute at-home quest, a quiet observation walk, a sensory-friendly group task, translation of public information, and an in-person build day should all be equally legitimate ways to begin. Never require public-facing work, spending money, unsafe labor, or personal disclosure to make progress.

## Sample starter questline: Less Food Waste

1. Learn common food-waste sources and distinguish avoidable waste from safety-required disposal.
2. With consent, track an anonymous baseline in a home or participating organization.
3. Test one low-risk prevention adjustment without compromising food safety or worker conditions.
4. Review results with staff and affected people; publish only aggregated, permissioned findings.
5. If promising, propose an accountable and reversible operations pilot, then share a replicable playbook.

**Stop condition:** If a quest risks privacy, food safety, labor rights, or retaliation, do not proceed until its design is changed or proper oversight is obtained.
